# Playbook 6: Keyword mapping, SEO clean-up and landing pages

## Keyword research that ends in decisions

For a keyword project I combined the keyword-planner export (thousands of ideas, filtered down to the commercially relevant few) with a search-console export of the site's own query and page data. The output wasn't a keyword dump, it was a **map**: one row per cluster, with local search volume, intent, a bid-value proxy, the target page and the current status.

What the map found:

- **Keyword cannibalisation.** One high-impression term was spread across three pages, at different positions, and getting no clicks. The fix is to pick a winner, redirect or re-point the others, and rewrite the winner's title and description, *before* writing anything new.
- **Demand with no page.** Three of the highest-demand local clusters (including the highest-value-per-click one) had no dedicated service page. The next build list came straight from this.
- **Traffic that would dead-end.** The best organic page was built around a government grant that had since closed. The recommendation was to keep the page but repoint it to "what can I build now" and route visitors to the live offer.
- **Honest limits.** One data source (a third-party keyword-difficulty tool) wasn't available, and the map says so instead of guessing.

## On-page clean-up I carried out or specified

- Replaced a retired phone number across all the pages that still showed it.
- Found pages printing a **second meta description** because of leftover SEO-plugin database rows, deleted them, then purged caches and tested with a click-ID in the URL.
- Wrote title and meta proposals page by page, with an audit file showing duplicates and gaps.
- Reviewed and cross-checked website copy for claims that couldn't be verified, and produced a corrections checklist.
- Fixed trailing-slash URL variants in ad final URLs.

## Landing pages built for a single audience

Separate conversion pages for each audience (basement suite, whole-home renovation, investor, and the planned kitchen and bathroom pages) instead of one generic page. Each has one clear action, the form where the eye lands, proof close to the form, and tracking that was tested end to end before ads were pointed at it.

I also fed review language and real search phrases back into ad copy: a longer set of responsive-search-ad headlines and descriptions, aligned with the keyword each ad group targets (checked with an ad-to-keyword alignment script).
