# Case study 2: Brand authority and a content system in a crowded construction market

*A design-build renovation company in Ontario, Canada. The client is anonymised.*

## Objective

Make a regional construction brand credible and recognisable to **two audiences, homeowners and property investors**, in a market where every competitor claims the same things. Build the awareness and trust that the lead-generation campaigns rely on.

## Strategy

**Compete on proof, not on knocking competitors.** I built the positioning around what the company can actually show: a provincial new-home warranty registration, a top-tier public rating, an industry award, fixed-price contracts and full permit management. The rule was that content speaks only to what the company does and lets the audience draw their own conclusions.

I also **split the voice by audience**: outcome-first homeowner content on the brand channels, and a separate investor-facing track on the founder's professional profile.

## Execution

- **A content system built from scratch:** a rolling 30-day calendar across Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube and Pinterest; a master strategy document; and a brand guide covering voice, content pillars, banned language, ideal customer profiles and per-platform rules.
- **Creative direction:** briefed carousel and reel packages for named project build-series, wrote the copy for each platform, and directed designers and video editors against the brief.
- **A voice-note-to-post workflow,** so I could build the founder's professional content without taking up their time.
- **Authority anchored on real milestones,** including a public multi-unit groundbreaking attended by local officials, with comment-keyword lead capture on the posts.
- **Claim discipline:** only verified claims were allowed in copy, and a banned-words list kept the tone calm and specific.

## Results

This is a brand and awareness layer, so I measured it on **engagement, authority signals and comment-to-lead capture**, not direct revenue. It gave the company a consistent, credible presence across five platforms where it had none, and turned project milestones into repeatable content and inbound conversations.

## Learnings

- **Credential-led content did better than promotional content.** People responded to proof (permits, registrations, finished builds) more than to claims.
- **Audience discipline matters.** Homeowner and investor content need different messages. Mixing them weakened both, so I split them onto separate channels.
- **Differentiate by the combination.** Competitors matched individual trust signals one at a time; none matched the whole stack. See [Playbook 4](../playbooks/04-meta-creative-testing.md).
